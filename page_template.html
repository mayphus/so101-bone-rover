Bone Rover

A generative-design frame for the SO-101 arm, shown with the official SO-101 model on top. Two encoder motors drive 100 mm wheels at the back, and one rubber swivel caster carries the nose, so the base rolls on pavement and short grass. The frame also holds the battery, a Waveshare driver board and an overview camera on a mast. Every strut grew where a load runs from the arm down to the wheels. It prints as 4 pieces, joined where a strut crosses a cut: the strut swells into a rounded knuckle with M3 screws inside, grown in the same field as the rest of the bone.

No depth camera needed. LeRobot policies such as ACT learn well from two plain USB cameras: one on the wrist and one overhead. Load cases the chassis was optimized for

Arm weight

Straight down on the top pad.

Reach forward

Pitch moment: front of the pad pushed down, back pulled up.

Reach sideways

Roll moment across the pad, mirrored left and right.

Base rotation

Yaw twist when the shoulder servo accelerates.

Push and brake

Horizontal pad loads from driving and bumping.

Motor torque

Drive reaction twisting each motor face plate.

Reach backward

Reverse pitch, toward the mast.

Battery, boards, motors

Battery hanging from the straps, driver and Pi on the deck, motor weight on the rear cradles.

Caster plate

The caster's steel plate pushing up equally on every bolt boss, so each boss gets its own strut.

Camera mast

Sideways knock on the tall mast, a twisting couple on its socket.

Supports: three points on the ground. Each wheel is a hinge on its shaft, and the caster bolt bosses hold vertical load. Only the bosses were fixed; the optimizer grew everything between them. Motors, encoder plugs, wheel wells, the caster swing circle and the battery bay were kept clear.

The frame is far stronger than these loads need. Strength isn't the limit; printability is (members can't go much thinner than about 5 mm). Print with 2 walls and 10–15% infill. The knuckle joints are modeled as bonded. Infill lowers real stiffness by roughly half, which still leaves a large margin.

Assembly

  1. Print the rear and front bodies upright with tree supports; print the two motor pods with the face plate flat on the bed.
  2. Press M3 heat-set inserts into the knuckle faces at the cuts (the smaller holes), M5 inserts into the arm pad, M4 inserts under the caster pad, M3 inserts in the driver bosses.
  3. Slide each motor in through its pod until the gearbox face meets the plate; six M3 × 6 through the plate. Plug the encoder cable in first: there is a cable space between the two motors.
  4. Bolt each pod and the front body to the rear body through the knuckles (M3 × 10; longer ones bottom out).
  5. Wheels onto the shafts; tighten the coupling set screws on the D-flat. Caster on with four M4 × 8.
  6. Strap the battery up into the bay under the arm. Driver board on 6 mm standoffs on the front deck; Pi on 20 mm standoffs above it if you use one.
  7. SO-101 on the pad with four M5 countersunk screws from inside its base. Mast into the tail socket, camera head on top.
  8. Wrist camera: remove the gripper servo (motor 6), drop two M3 hex nuts into the wrist roll part, refit the servo, then screw on the official mount and the camera (4 × M2). Set focus by twisting the lens.

Check before you print

  • Motor. Six M3 on a 31 mm circle, shaft 7 mm off center. Gearbox length, can length, encoder board and plug position vary by seller: check GEARBOX_L, CAN_L, ENC_L, PLUG_* and FACE_HOLE_ANGLE0 in params.py.
  • Wheel. Set for 100 × 40 mm. For rougher ground a 130 mm off-road wheel fits after raising WHEEL_D; the frame regenerates.
  • Caster. Set for a 64 mm tall 2" caster. A taller caster tilts the base back; change CASTER_H to match yours.
  • Arm mount. The pad matches the official SO-101 follower base (base_so101_v2): four M5 holes.
  • Tipping. With 3 kg total, the base stays stable with the arm fully out forward or sideways (200 g payload; about 26 mm margin sideways). Fully reaching backward past the axle with a load can tip it over the rear wheels, so keep the work area in front, where the camera looks anyway.
